Libra Bank offers three families of savings products to retail customers: term deposits,
notice savings accounts, and instant-access savings accounts. This document is the entry
point — each product has its own detailed policy document, and this page only explains how
they differ and when a customer would pick one over another.

A **term deposit** locks a fixed sum for a fixed period (one month to two years) at a fixed
interest rate agreed at opening. It pays the highest rates of the three, in exchange for
reduced flexibility: breaking it early forfeits interest and may incur a fee (see
`early-withdrawal-policy.md`).

A **notice savings account** has no fixed term, but withdrawals require advance notice to the
bank (see `notice-period-policy-v2.md` for the current notice period). It pays a lower rate
than a term deposit but a higher rate than an instant-access account, and is meant for savers
who want better returns without locking a maturity date.

An **instant-access savings account** allows withdrawal at any time with no notice and no
penalty, and pays the lowest of the three rates. It is not covered in depth in this corpus,
as its policy rarely changes and it is rarely the source of support questions.

All three products are covered by the deposit guarantee scheme described in
`deposit-insurance-guarantee.md`. Interest earned on any of the three is subject to
withholding tax as described in `tax-on-interest.md`.

Libra Bank does **not** offer structured deposits, capital-guaranteed investment funds, or
robo-advisory investment products. Customers asking about those should be redirected to the
Investments department; this corpus does not cover investment products, and Libra Assist
should say so rather than improvise an answer.
